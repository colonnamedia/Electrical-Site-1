When there is immediate danger

Move away from the hazard.

If you see fire or smoke, exposed wiring, arcing, a downed line, or a shock risk, keep clear and call local emergency services or the utility as appropriate. Do not use a website form for an immediate hazard.

This sample site does not monitor requests or offer emergency dispatch. A purchased site is connected and tested with your business contact and service process before launch.

Two customer routes

Homes and businesses ask different questions.

Residential customers can describe rooms, devices, and timing. Commercial customers can identify facility type, operating constraints, tenant coordination, and the best point of contact.

  • Residential repairs, renovations, panels, lighting, generators, and EV readiness
  • Commercial troubleshooting, tenant improvements, lighting, dedicated circuits, and maintenance planning
  • Actual code, permit, warranty, and inspection language supplied by the contractor

What to include

Give the contractor a head start.

  • The affected room, circuit or equipment
  • When the issue started and what changed
  • Your property type and service location
  • Access, operating hours and preferred timing
  • A phone number or email for a follow-up
